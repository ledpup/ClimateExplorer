ACORN-SAT version 2.7 was released in October 2026, and ClimateExplorer now uses it in place of version 2.6. It has daily temperature records up to the end of 2025 for the 112 locations around Australia in ACORN-SAT.

ACORN-SAT is homogenised: the Bureau of Meteorology adjusts each location's earlier records so they're consistent with how temperature is measured there now, for example after a site move or a change of instruments. A new release can add, reassess or remove adjustments. When an adjustment changes, every year before it shifts, while the years after it stay the same.

Version 2.7 has new or reassessed adjustments at 20 of the 112 locations (the Bureau's statement is below). Comparing the two versions, daily mean temperatures for 2010–2019 moved by more than 0.05°C at 13 locations, some up and some down. The largest changes were at [Oodnadatta](https://climateexplorer.net/location/oodnadatta) (about 0.4°C warmer) and [Tennant Creek](https://climateexplorer.net/location/tennant-creek) (about 0.4°C cooler).

## Canberra

[Canberra](https://climateexplorer.net/location/canberra) is one of the locations that changed. In version 2.7, every year before 2020 is about 0.15–0.2°C warmer for mean temperature, and about 0.3–0.4°C warmer for maximum temperature. Records from 2020 onwards are unchanged.

Raising the earlier years but not the recent ones flattens the recent trend. Canberra's trend in yearly mean temperature over the last 30 years (1996–2025) went from 0.35°C per decade in version 2.6 to 0.29°C per decade in version 2.7. That's still a statistically significant warming trend, at a rate of nearly 0.3°C per decade.

The trend over a single 30-year window at a single location is sensitive to adjustments like this, and to unusual years at either end. Longer windows and averages across many locations are steadier. That fits the Bureau's finding that the changes have a negligible effect on Australia's long-term warming trend.

The [Bureau of Meteorology](https://www.bom.gov.au/) released a statement about version 2.7 on their [ACORN-SAT website](https://www.bom.gov.au/climate/data/acorn-sat/), reproduced below.

> ### New and reassessed adjustments as part of ACORN-SAT version 2.7
> ACORN-SAT 2.7 includes new or reassessed adjustments at 20 of the 112 ACORN-SAT locations. Previously applied adjustments at 5 locations were removed after being found to be no longer significant with the inclusion of additional data, and there were 9 new adjustments.

> Analysis has shown the changed adjustments in ACORN-SAT 2.7 have had a negligible impact on estimated long-term warming trend in Australia.

> Full details of all adjustments, including the time periods and reference periods used, are available in the station catalogue. The Bureau's ACORN-SAT dataset and methods have been thoroughly peer-reviewed and found to be world-leading. Further information on the dataset and the reasons for adjustments can be found in the ACORN-SAT [FAQ section](https://www.bom.gov.au/climate/data/acorn-sat/#tabs=FAQs).
